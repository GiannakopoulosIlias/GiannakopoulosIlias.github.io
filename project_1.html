MRI Coil Simulation and Design

I develop computational methods that make radiofrequency coil simulation and design faster, more scalable, and more accessible. My work spans tensor compression, integral-equation solvers, ideal current analysis, and open-source tools for anatomy-specific coil engineering.

IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation · 2019

Memory Footprint Reduction for the FFT-Based Volume Integral Equation Method via Tensor Decompositions

This work introduced tensor decompositions to compress the large operators used in electromagnetic MRI simulations. The resulting memory savings made high-resolution, GPU-accelerated coil–body simulations far more practical.

IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation · 2022

2023 Harold A. Wheeler Prize Paper Award

Compression of Volume-Surface Integral Equation Matrices via Tucker Decomposition for Magnetic Resonance Applications

This study extended tensor compression to the electromagnetic coupling between RF coils and anatomical models. It removed a major memory bottleneck in detailed MRI simulations while preserving the underlying physics.

Read the paper

IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering · 2023

A Hybrid Volume-Surface Integral Equation Method for Rapid Electromagnetic Simulations in MRI

This work combined complementary fast solvers for nearby RF coils and large scanner structures. The hybrid approach made comprehensive simulations of realistic MRI systems substantially more feasible.

Read the paper

Magnetic Resonance in Medicine · 2023

Computational Methods for the Estimation of Ideal Current Patterns in Realistic Human Models

This work developed practical ways to estimate the best RF current patterns that could theoretically be achieved for realistic human anatomy. These ideal patterns provide useful performance benchmarks and guidance for future coil designs.

An Open-Source Software Toolbox for Rapid Radiofrequency Coil Design and Evaluation in MRI

This project brought our simulation, circuit modeling, and optimization methods together in an open-source toolbox. It makes advanced, anatomy-specific RF coil engineering more accessible to researchers and coil designers.
